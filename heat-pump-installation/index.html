A heat pump is an air conditioner that learned to run in reverse: in summer it moves heat out of your house, and in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow so the same compressor pulls heat from outdoor air and delivers it inside. Because it moves heat rather than generating it, its efficiency runs far past anything resistance heating can do, which is why so many Aloha, OR homeowners are switching as older equipment ages out. Modern cold-climate models with variable-speed inverter compressors keep producing useful heat far below freezing, a genuine engineering leap over the heat pumps of twenty years ago.

The design phase decides whether a heat pump lives up to its promise. Unlike a furnace or an AC alone, it must handle both the cooling load and the heating load, so we calculate each one and locate the balance point, the outdoor temperature below which the unit needs help. From there we match compressor staging to your climate and ducts and design the backup heat: electric strips that stage in only when needed, or a dual-fuel pairing where a gas furnace handles the coldest stretches. Electrical capacity and duct airflow get verified too, because the lower supply temperature of a heat pump only feels right with enough air moving.

On installation day we follow the same standards used on every Aloha HVAC system. The line set is pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, the charge is weighed in and checked with superheat and subcooling, and the outdoor unit goes on a stand wherever winter defrost drainage requires it. Condensate is handled properly, and the thermostat is configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost all work as designed. Why Professional Heat Pump Installation Matters

Since a heat pump runs in both seasons, installation mistakes cost you twice. An incorrect charge cuts cooling efficiency in summer and heating capacity in winter. Skip the balance point calculation and electric backup strips may end up heating the house at several times the operating cost, leaving the homeowner puzzled about why the bills never dropped. Leave the thermostat on factory defaults and it can call for backup heat the compressor could have handled, or interfere with defrost. Set the outdoor unit flat on the ground in a real winter and it sits in refrozen defrost water until the coil turns to ice.

Our experienced technicians address each of those risks by design. We calculate both seasonal loads, choose equipment for its real capacity curve at your local winter design temperature, and configure backup heat staging and lockouts so the strips or furnace only run when they should. The outdoor unit is elevated to drain its defrost water, and the charge is weighed in and verified. At startup we commission heating and cooling separately, confirm the reversing valve, and watch a defrost cycle finish. With federally regulated refrigerant work and inverter electronics involved, precision matters, and it is the installation that decides whether a heat pump is a delight or a disappointment.

Heat Pump Installation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Watch the first winter bill; a sudden spike usually means backup heat is overworking
  • Give the outdoor unit open space all year, free of drifts and overgrowth
  • Keep a regular filter schedule so the system has enough air in summer and winter
  • Schedule spring and fall maintenance to keep the charge and defrost checked
  • Keep temperature settings steady so the backup strips are not needed to catch up
  • Light frost and an occasional defrost hiss are normal in winter, but solid ice calls for service
  • Gently hose off the outdoor coil every spring before cooling season
