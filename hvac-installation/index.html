Where do most HVAC installations go wrong? Usually before the first wrench turns, when someone matches the new tonnage to the old nameplate and skips the math. At Aloha HVAC, the math comes first. We build a load calculation for your Aloha, OR home from its square footage, insulation levels, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because sizing errors in either direction cost comfort and money every day the system runs. From there the job becomes three connected pieces of work: equipment matched to the load, ductwork able to deliver what that equipment produces, and a commissioning process that proves the result with real readings instead of on paper.

Matching is the part of equipment selection most homeowners never hear about. The coil, the condenser, and the blower carry efficiency ratings as a combination, and when the pieces are mixed, the SEER2 and AFUE numbers you paid for simply are not there. So we size to the load and then walk through the choices honestly: single-stage versus two-stage versus variable-speed, ECM blowers versus conventional motors, and whether your home is better served by a furnace, an air handler, or a heat pump. Ductwork is the other half of the story, and most installers skip it entirely. We measure static pressure, find the leaks and undersized branches, and correct them while the system is apart, because a duct system that fights the equipment will beat it every time.

The install itself is a checklist, not a guess: line sets pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed by superheat and subcooling, gas connections leak-tested, venting run to manufacturer spec, condensate trapped and protected by a float switch, and the thermostat configured for the equipment's actual staging. We finish with full commissioning, recording temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, then walk you through everything before we haul the old equipment away. Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Installation?

An HVAC system is the largest machine in most homes, and installation is where its entire service life gets decided. The failure modes are invisible at handoff: a vacuum never verified means moisture breeding acid in the refrigerant loop, a charge set by feel means efficiency lost for good, mismatched coil-and-condenser pairings mean the rated numbers were never achievable, and duct restrictions mean the blower spends fifteen years straining against static pressure it was never designed for. Refrigerant work is federally regulated and gas and venting mistakes are safety problems, not just performance problems.

Our experienced technicians treat installation as a chain of proofs, not a list of tasks. The load calculation proves the size. Matched equipment makes the rating reachable. A pressure test and a micron-gauge vacuum prove the circuit is tight and dry, while a weighed charge checked against superheat and subcooling proves the refrigerant side. On the heating side, gas lines are leak-tested, draft is verified, and blower speed is set to hold the rated temperature rise. Finally, commissioning puts the numbers on paper, creating the baseline every future visit is measured against. That documentation is standard on every system we install in Aloha.

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Installation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Change filters on schedule from day one; airflow protects both the coil and the exchanger
  • Give the outdoor unit clear space on every side and a gentle rinse each season
  • Address small noises and performance drifts early, while they are still small
  • Hold onto your commissioning readings; they make every future diagnosis faster and cheaper
  • Keep supply and return vents open to preserve the airflow balance set at commissioning
  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Schedule two tune-ups a year: cooling in spring, heating in fall
